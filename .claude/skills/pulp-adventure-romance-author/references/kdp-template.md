# Amazon KDP Metadata — Pulp Adventure Romance

## Title (max 200 characters)

Should signal action, romance, and adventure within the first three words. Series titles include subtitle with book number.

**Format:** `[Hook Title]: [Subtitle Optional]` or `[Title] (The [Series] Book [Number])`

Avoid:
- Generic noun pairings ("Heart of Stone")
- Single-word titles unless distinctively unusual
- Titles that signal pure romance with no adventure cue

Reach for:
- Place + peril + romantic suggestion ("The Cartagena Cipher")
- Verb + object combinations ("Stealing the Sun Stone")
- Antagonist name + protagonist relationship ("The Magdalena Pact")

## Subtitle

Optional but recommended for KDP discoverability. Use to signal genre and stakes:

> "A Pulp Adventure Romance" — explicit genre marker
> "An Expedition Romance" — softer
> "A [Setting] Adventure Romance" — geographic anchor

## Author Name

As specified in Phase 2 setup. If a pen name, do not deviate.

## Book Description (max 4,000 characters)

Standard structure (adapt voice):

```
[Hook line — 1 sentence that sets the McGuffin and the romantic spark in one breath]

[Protagonist A introduction — 2-3 sentences. Profession, current state, what they're after, why it matters to them personally]

[Protagonist B introduction — 2-3 sentences. Profession, why they collide with A, the specific friction]

[Setup paragraph — 3-4 sentences. The expedition, the McGuffin, the antagonist's pressure, the impossible stakes]

[Romance promise paragraph — 2-3 sentences. The trope, the heat level, the obstacle, the implied HEA without spoiling]

[Comp line — "Perfect for readers of [Comp 1] and [Comp 2]" or "For fans of [Film 1] meets [Author]"]

[Optional content notes for Spicy heat]
```

**Tone calibration:**
- Use present tense for action description
- Past tense for backstory framing
- Vivid verbs over stacked adjectives
- Specific locale, not "exotic locales"

## Comp Titles (3)

Include both contemporary and legacy comps. Format:

`[Title] by [Author] (Year)`

Use comp titles that:
- Sit in the same heat lane (no Sweet comp for a Spicy book)
- Sit in the same era cluster
- Have the same protagonist configuration
- Are within the last 5–7 years for at least 2 of the 3

Sample comp pool (calibrate to era and heat):
- Contemporary: Adventure romance bestsellers from the past 5 years
- Classic Pulp: Mary Stewart, Helen MacInnes (era-appropriate; respect the legacy)
- Treasure-hunt mid-list contemporaries with adventure romance flavor

## Audience Profile

- **Age category:** Adult
- **Reading level:** Adult genre fiction
- **Primary demographic:** Romance readers seeking adventure, adventure readers open to central romance
- **Secondary demographic:** Crossover from romantic suspense, historical romance, archaeological mystery
- **Heat-level audience marker:** Always state heat level explicitly in the description for Spicy books

## Cover Image Prompt

Era-specific visual anchors:

### Classic Pulp (1920s–40s)
- Color palette: warm browns, deep greens, parchment, brass
- Typography: serif display fonts; pulp magazine-era nameplate styling
- Imagery: silhouetted figures against architectural ruins, era-appropriate clothing (handle period-accuracy with care; avoid colonial cliché)
- Mood: cinematic, painterly, classic

### Mid-Century
- Color palette: navy, ochre, faded red
- Typography: clean serif, mid-century film-poster influence
- Imagery: figures with iconic gear (binoculars, dive masks, crashed aircraft)
- Mood: cool, propulsive, nostalgic

### Contemporary
- Color palette: rich saturated tones, modern photographic feel
- Typography: clean sans-serif or strong contemporary serif
- Imagery: figures with modern adventure gear, evocative locale background
- Mood: cinematic, current, urgent

### Retro-Futurist
- Color palette: brass, oxblood, ozone blue
- Typography: art-deco-influenced display
- Imagery: airships, ray guns, diesel-punk machinery, alternate-history flags
- Mood: stylized, atmospheric, distinctive
