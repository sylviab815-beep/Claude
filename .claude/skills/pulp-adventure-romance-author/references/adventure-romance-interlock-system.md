# Adventure-Romance Interlock System (ARIS) — Author Reference

This is the writing-stage version of the ARIS guardrail. The Outline Builder applied ARIS structurally; the Author applies ARIS at the prose level, every chapter.

## The Six Rules at the Prose Level

### Rule 1: Causal Crossover

**At the prose level:** Inside the chapter, find at least one moment where action progress requires emotional progress, or vice versa. The two threads must visibly braid in the language of the scene, not just in the chapter summary.

**Prose check:** Can you point to one paragraph where the action and the romance are on the same line of cause-and-effect?

**Common failure mode:** Two scenes glued together. A chase scene followed by a tender scene where the chase had nothing to do with the tenderness. Solution: rewrite the transition so one scene caused the other.

### Rule 2: Pulp Set-Piece Cadence

**At the prose level:** Maintain pulp's heart rate in the sentences themselves. Long languid paragraphs in the middle of a set-piece break cadence. Equally — pure staccato through a quiet character beat feels off-genre.

**Prose check:** Read the chapter aloud. Does the rhythm match the beat type the outline assigned (chase = tighter sentences, breath = longer with more clauses)?

**Common failure mode:** Defaulting to one rhythm across all chapters. Solution: calibrate sentence-length distribution per Pulp Beat Type.

### Rule 3: Vulnerability Under Threat

**At the prose level:** When intimacy lands, the threat must be on the page — present, sensory, near. Not a memory of threat. Not a worry about threat. Active.

**Prose check:** In any intimate scene, is there a sensory detail of threat within the scene itself? (Tracker dogs barking in the distance. Water rising. Footsteps on the staircase. Time on a clock visible in the corner.)

**Common failure mode:** Threat is told (he knew they didn't have long) but not shown. Solution: anchor the threat in a sensory detail within the scene.

### Rule 4: McGuffin Gravity

**At the prose level:** The chapter's prose itself should make the McGuffin feel present. References, weight, anticipation, resistance. The reader should not need a recap to remember what's at stake.

**Prose check:** Without reading any other chapter, can a reader of this chapter identify what is being chased and why?

**Common failure mode:** Chapter happens "off the chase" — character backstory chapter with no McGuffin contact. Solution: tie the backstory to the McGuffin (a memory triggered by the artifact, a connection between the family and the relic, a buried clue).

### Rule 5: Banter-Action Equivalence

**At the prose level:** Both protagonists speak under pressure. Their voices stay distinct in dialogue. Neither falls silent unless silence is a deliberate, earned beat.

**Prose check:** In any pressure scene, count lines of dialogue from each protagonist. If one is silent for the duration, was that a choice?

**Common failure mode:** Action sequences become stage directions with one or no voices. Solution: drop one to two sharp exchanges into every set-piece.

### Rule 6: Dual-Currency Escalation

**At the prose level:** Each chapter's closing image should signal raised stakes in both currencies. Physical situation worse. Emotional exposure deeper. Both, together.

**Prose check:** Compare the chapter's opening situation to the closing image. Is the protagonist physically more cornered? Emotionally more exposed?

**Common failure mode:** Chapter ends on a great cliffhanger — but only physical. Or a great emotional beat — but the chase didn't move. Solution: rework the closing image to compress both.

## ARIS Intervention Library

When a rule fails, apply the matching intervention.

| Failure | Intervention |
|---------|--------------|
| Two parallel threads, no crossover | Rewrite a transition: the action's resolution must require an emotional move. |
| Quiet chapter with no McGuffin gravity | Inject a McGuffin reference that triggers the character beat (memory, object, message, sensation). |
| Intimacy with no on-page threat | Add an active sensory threat anchor in the scene (sound, time pressure, proximity). |
| One protagonist silent under pressure | Insert one to two sharp dialogue exchanges that accomplish characterization, romance, and pacing. |
| Single-currency act break | Rework the closing image to raise both physical and emotional stakes simultaneously. |
| McGuffin forgotten for two chapters | Insert a McGuffin pressure beat (rival close, deadline tightens, new clue surfaces). |

## Manuscript-Level Audit (Stage 2 of Editorial)

After the draft is complete, audit the manuscript:

- **McGuffin Gravity Curve:** Plot McGuffin Gravity (direct / pressure / shadow / zero) across all chapters. No gaps of two consecutive chapters at zero.
- **Act Break Currencies:** At each act break, both currencies should leap. If not, rewrite.
- **Black Moment Currencies:** Black moment must hit both currencies in the same scene.
- **Climax Choice:** Climax should be a moment where a character chooses between the prize and the love (or refuses the false choice through earned cleverness).
- **HEA / HFN Currencies:** Both must be paid — relationship sealed AND McGuffin question answered.

If the manuscript fails any audit point, the chapter or scene must be rewritten before the editorial pipeline continues.
