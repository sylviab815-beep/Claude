# Pulp Adventure Romance Prose Craft Rules

Ten mandatory rules. Apply during drafting (1–6) and during editorial revision (7–10 plus full re-pass).

## Rule 1: Pulp Sentence Rhythm

Pulp adventure runs on a faster heart rate than contemporary romance and tighter than thriller. Sentences vary deliberately by Pulp Beat Type:

- **Chase / ambush:** Mostly short. Verbs. Movement first, then consequence.
- **Reveal / discovery:** Medium-length, sensory, building toward the line that lands the reveal.
- **Breath / aftermath:** Longer, with subordinate clauses that let the reader exhale.
- **Intimate beats:** Lyrical when heat allows; sharp and clipped when threat dominates.

Vary rhythm within scenes; never default to one length. AI's failure mode is "uniform medium" sentences across every beat.

**Check:** Read the chapter aloud. Does sentence length shift with the beat?

## Rule 2: Sensory Specificity in Locale

Exotic locales fail when rendered as set-dressing. Every location must have at least three concrete sensory anchors that could not be transposed to another setting:

- A specific sound (cicada-song frequency, the hull-creak of a particular ship)
- A specific smell (kerosene + jasmine in the night market, salt-rotted timber)
- A texture or temperature anchor (slate gritty with cave dust, the cold bite of altitude)

Avoid generic adjective stacks ("hot, dusty, ancient"). Avoid the AI three-beat description default.

**Check:** Could you swap this locale for any other and the prose still work? If yes, you have failed this rule.

## Rule 3: Banter Under Threat

Both protagonists speak when cornered. Their dialogue under pressure must accomplish three things at once: characterization (their voice), romance (their dynamic), pacing (compression of the scene).

Avoid:
- Stage-direction dialogue ("Hurry!" "I'm hurrying!")
- Generic action exclamations
- Long monologues mid-set-piece

Reach for:
- Sharp specificity (he calls her by the name only he uses, in the worst possible moment)
- Mid-action vulnerability (she admits something she would never admit if there were time to think)
- Voice contrast (her register vs his — keep them distinct)

**Check:** In every pressure scene, count dialogue lines per protagonist. Both must speak unless silence is a chosen beat.

## Rule 4: Set-Piece Scene Architecture

Pulp set-pieces follow a structural pattern. Use it consciously:

1. **Approach** — recognize the threat is coming
2. **Engagement** — the threat lands
3. **Escalation** — it gets worse, an unforeseen element joins
4. **Crisis Choice** — a decision that costs
5. **Resolution** — escape, capture, partial win, or pyrrhic
6. **Aftermath** — physical inventory + emotional cost

Skipping the Crisis Choice flattens the scene. The choice is where romance can re-enter the action — what one protagonist sacrifices to save the other.

**Check:** Does every set-piece scene contain a Crisis Choice that costs?

## Rule 5: Romantic Vulnerability Calibration

Vulnerability beats are calibrated to heat level:

- **Sweet:** Eye contact held too long. The hand on the wound. The uninvited reading of a letter.
- **Steamy:** Sensory immersion in attraction without explicit detail. The first kiss in the rain. The press of forehead to forehead.
- **Spicy:** On-page intimate detail. Heat is character revelation, not anatomical inventory.

Per ARIS Rule 3, vulnerability lands under threat. Do not stage intimacy in safe places with no clock.

**Check:** Does every romantic beat have an active threat anchor in the scene?

## Rule 6: Heat-Level Compliance

The heat level set in Phase 2 setup is binding. Do not drift up or down across the manuscript. Specifically:

- Sweet manuscripts fade to black with strong emotional payoff. No on-page explicit detail.
- Steamy manuscripts use sensory immersion without graphic specificity. No clinical anatomy.
- Spicy manuscripts use on-page explicit detail but always in service of emotional escalation.

If the outline marked a chapter as containing an intimate beat at heat level X, write at heat level X.

**Check:** Does every intimate beat match the chosen heat level exactly?

## Rule 7: Dialogue Voice Distinction

Both protagonists must be identifiable by dialogue alone. Build voice profiles before drafting:

- Vocabulary register (formal, casual, technical, regional)
- Sentence length tendency
- Rhythm (does the character interrupt, hedge, declare, ask)
- Signature words (rare — three to five only — and never overused)

In dialogue scenes, blind-test by removing dialogue tags. Could a reader still identify who is speaking?

**Check:** Strip tags from a dialogue scene. Can you still tell who's who?

## Rule 8: Genre Authenticity

A Pulp Adventure Romance reader recognizes the genre from page one. Markers:

- The opening beat establishes McGuffin pursuit OR meet-under-threat (not a quiet domestic morning)
- The voice has propulsive momentum from the first paragraph
- The first chapter contains at least one piece of physical risk and at least one piece of romantic spark
- The locale is rendered with specificity from first description

If the first chapter could be lifted into a contemporary romance with no other changes, you have failed this rule.

**Check:** Does the first chapter feel unmistakably pulp adventure romance?

## Rule 9: AI Cliché Avoidance

Pulp Adventure Romance has its own AI cliché basket. Strip these:

- "Their eyes met across the [chamber / crater / battlefield / market]"
- "[She/he] couldn't help but notice [physical attribute]"
- "A shiver ran down [pronoun] spine"
- "Time seemed to slow"
- "In that moment, [protagonist] knew"
- "The air crackled with tension"
- "Heart pounding in [pronoun] chest" used more than once
- "Adrenaline coursed through [pronoun]"
- "[Protagonist] couldn't have prepared for what came next"
- Three-item descriptive lists in every locale ("hot, dry, ancient")
- "Ancient evil," "ancient secret," "ancient power" without specific noun
- "Forbidden [city / temple / language / love]" without ironic purpose
- "Whispered promises of [abstract noun]"

Replace with specific, scene-anchored language.

**Check:** Search the manuscript for every phrase above. Eliminate or replace each instance.

## Rule 10: McGuffin Specificity

The McGuffin is the thematic spine of the book. Render it specifically — not as "a mysterious artifact" but as a particular object with weight, history, sound, smell, and consequence.

If the McGuffin is interchangeable with any other, the book is interchangeable with any other.

The McGuffin's specificity should also illuminate at least one protagonist's interior. Why does THIS object matter to THIS character beyond its monetary or historical value?

**Check:** Could the McGuffin be replaced with a different artifact and the book read the same? If yes, you have failed this rule.
