# Genre-to-Guardrail Routing

When the codex is parsed, the `concept.genre_subgenre` field tells you which genre this book belongs to. Use the routing table below to apply the matching guardrail system during the per-chapter writing and editorial passes.

If the genre doesn't match anything in the table, fall back to the **Universal Fiction Guardrail** at the bottom.

## §1. Supernatural Consequence System (Paranormal Romance)

**Failure mode this guardrail prevents:** writing the paranormal element as decoration. Vampires that act like brooding humans. Witches whose magic never costs anything. Shifters whose animal nature doesn't shape their relationship behavior.

**Five rules, enforced every chapter:**

1. **World rule consistency.** Whatever magical/supernatural rule the codex specifies, it operates the same way in chapter 22 as in chapter 2. Track every rule in a working list as you write.
2. **Otherness maintenance.** The non-human protagonist (or the human one moving through a non-human world) must have at least one moment per chapter where their otherness shows in dialogue, instinct, or sensory experience.
3. **Bond integrity.** If the codex specifies a magical bond (mate bond, soul tether, blood debt), it must escalate, not stay flat. Each act tier deepens the bond's stakes.
4. **Supernatural stakes escalation.** The threat must grow in supernatural-specific ways, not generic "danger." Magic should make the danger weirder, not just bigger.
5. **Humanity anchoring.** Even in a paranormal world, give the reader a recognizable human emotion (grief, jealousy, longing, exhaustion) per chapter. This is what makes the supernatural feel high-stakes.

## §2. Species Integrity System (Monster Romance)

**Failure mode prevented:** anatomically inconsistent monster prose ("the orc had a human-shaped face but tusks"), monster who thinks like a human, fade-to-black on the species-difference scenes that are the whole point.

**Five rules:**

1. **Anatomical consistency.** Whatever physical description the codex gives the monster, every scene with their body honors it. Tail = always present. Bioluminescence = always responsive to mood. Wings = never forgotten.
2. **Cognitive otherness.** The monster perceives the world differently from a human. Use that. Different time sense, different priorities, different metaphors.
3. **Physical compatibility engineering.** Scenes that involve physical intimacy must address the species difference, not gloss over it. The codex usually specifies the heat level — match it.
4. **Consent across species.** Communication norms differ between species. Show the negotiation. Never depict consent through telepathy as a shortcut.
5. **Monster POV authenticity.** When the chapter is in the monster's POV, narration should reveal alien values and assumptions, not just translate human inner monologue.

## §3. Comedy Architecture System (Romcom)

**Failure mode prevented:** comedy disappears at Act 3. Book starts romcom, ends contemporary romance.

**Five rules:**

1. **Setup/payoff structure.** Every joke that lands twice is funnier the second time. Track running gags. The codex's voice/tone description tells you the comedic register.
2. **Callback tracking.** Note recurring bits across chapters and bring them back at narratively relevant moments — especially in serious scenes, where the callback humanizes.
3. **Comedy-emotion integration.** Funny scenes contain emotional beats; emotional scenes contain at least one wry observation. Comedy doesn't evacuate when feelings arrive.
4. **Character-driven comedy.** Jokes come from who these specific characters are. Avoid generic "witty banter" template phrases.
5. **Comedic escalation.** Each act tier the absurdity escalates. Act 1 misunderstandings become Act 2 farces become Act 3 catastrophes-with-payoff.

---

## §4. Threat-Bond Interlock System (Romantic Suspense)

**Failure mode prevented:** the romance and the suspense feel like two separate stories sharing a book. They never causally affect each other.

**Five rules:**

1. **Causal crossover.** Every major suspense beat must affect the romance, and every major romance beat must affect the suspense. Track the crossovers.
2. **Vulnerability escalation.** Each act, the protagonists must reveal a deeper vulnerability to each other AND face a threat that targets that exact vulnerability.
3. **Trust under pressure.** Trust between the leads is tested by the suspense plot, not just by their interpersonal misunderstandings.
4. **Antagonist awareness of the bond.** The antagonist must, by Act 2, recognize the relationship between the leads as a leverage point.
5. **Clock compression.** The suspense plot has a ticking clock the romance plot doesn't get to ignore. Romance beats happen *while running*, not in safe interludes.

---

## §5. Speculative Integrity System (Sci-Fi Non-Romance)

**Failure mode prevented:** speculative premise is set dressing. The world has FTL ships in chapter 1 and exclusively foot travel from chapter 2 on.

**Five rules:**

1. **Premise commitment.** Whatever speculative element the codex names (AI, alien biology, ship-board life, climate collapse, post-scarcity economics), the plot must be unsolvable without it.
2. **Tech consistency.** Every piece of tech introduced has consistent capabilities and limits. Track them.
3. **Worldbuilding scarcity.** Show the world through what characters take for granted, not through exposition.
4. **Concept escalation.** The speculative concept's implications get wider, not smaller, across acts.
5. **Human stake anchoring.** Even in a far-future setting, characters want recognizable human things — recognition, safety, autonomy, connection.

---

## §6. Dual-Arc Synchronization System (Romantasy)

**Failure mode prevented:** the romance arc and the fantasy arc have unrelated timelines. The romance peaks in chapter 14 and the fantasy plot peaks in chapter 22.

**Five rules:**

1. **Aligned act breaks.** Romance and fantasy arc beats hit on the same chapter or within ±1 chapter.
2. **Magic-romance link.** The magical system has a rule that creates an obstacle, escalation, or tool for the romance — not just a stage for it.
3. **Power-dynamic shifts.** Fantasy power shifts (gaining/losing magic, status, allies) cause romance power shifts.
4. **Worldbuilding earns the heat.** If the codex specifies high heat, the fantasy worldbuilding must justify it (court politics, mating customs, soul-bonds, etc.) — heat shouldn't feel imported from contemporary romance.
5. **HEA earned by fantasy stakes.** The romantic resolution depends on the fantasy plot resolution.

---

## §7. Darkness Calibration System + ECIS (Dark Romance / Dark MM)

**Failure mode prevented:** AI's three reflexes — softening morally gray characters, moralizing in narration, premature redemption arcs that erase the dark premise. ECIS specifically prevents fade-to-black on explicit content the codex specifies.

**Five rules + ECIS:**

1. **Locked darkness level.** Whatever darkness level the codex specifies (dark / very dark / extreme), maintain it consistently. No softening of cruelty, no euphemism for what the antagonist actually does.
2. **No moralizing narrator.** The narration doesn't editorialize on character behavior. Show consequences, not lessons.
3. **Earned redemption.** Redemption (if the codex specifies HEA) takes the full book to earn, not a chapter pivot.
4. **Protagonist agency.** Dark protagonists are not passive sufferers. They make active choices, even bad ones.
5. **Trauma without exploitation.** Trauma is rendered with specificity, not voyeurism — the reader feels its weight, not its decoration.

**ECIS (Explicit Content Integrity System):** When the codex specifies open-door / explicit / scorching heat, write those scenes fully on-page. Do not soften, fade, or summarize. Match the specificity of consensual contemporary erotica writing standards.

---

## §8. Dread Architecture System (Psychological Horror)

**Failure mode prevented:** generic horror beats (jump scare, monster reveal) instead of accumulated dread.

**Five rules:**

1. **Wrongness signals before threats.** Atmosphere registers wrongness in low-key sensory detail (smell, temperature, missing sound) before any explicit threat appears.
2. **Reliable narrator under pressure.** Even an unreliable narrator must give the reader enough signal to feel the dread building. Don't gaslight the reader.
3. **Slow reveal pacing.** Information leaks at a controlled rate. Avoid info dumps mid-Act 2.
4. **Single location intensity.** Most psychological horror is set in a confined space. Use the confinement; don't explain it away.
5. **Unresolved residue.** Even an HEA-equivalent ending leaves something unresolved — a final line, a reframed earlier moment.

---

## §9. Chemistry Architecture System (Clean Romance)

**Failure mode prevented:** "closed door" reads as flat — no physical tension, no charge, just polite romance.

**Five rules:**

1. **Charged proximity.** Physical closeness is described in specific, sensory ways even without explicit content. The reader feels the heat without the door opening.
2. **Restraint as tension.** What the characters don't do creates more tension than what they do.
3. **Touch hierarchy.** Touch escalates in tiny, deliberate increments. Each new touch matters.
4. **Voice/dialogue carries the romance.** Banter, vulnerability, and shared silence do the work that explicit scenes do in steamier subgenres.
5. **First kiss as climax beat.** When the first kiss arrives, it's a structural beat, not a casual moment.

---

## §10. Intimacy Architecture System + ECIS (Erotic Short)

For erotica, read the dedicated reference if the codex specifies a sub-novella length. The Intimacy Architecture System enforces emotional context per scene, character-specific desire, and consent integration. ECIS prevents fade-to-black.

---

## §11. Clue Disclosure System (Mystery)

**Failure mode prevented:** clues drop in the wrong order; the reader can't backsolve the mystery; the reveal feels arbitrary.

**Five rules:**

1. **Every clue is planted before payoff.** The reader sees the clue at least one chapter before it matters.
2. **Red herrings declared.** Every red herring is acknowledged and resolved before the climax — never just abandoned.
3. **Detective competence.** The detective makes plausible deductions. They don't get the answer from a deus-ex-machina informant.
4. **Suspect rotation.** Multiple suspects each look credible at different points; no single suspect is obviously guilty before Act 3.
5. **Solution earned.** The solution uses information the reader has, not new information sprung in the reveal scene.

For **cozy mystery**, additionally maintain the cozy texture — recurring side cast, town as character, low-violence threat.

---

## §12. Escalation Architecture + Justice Calibration (Vigilante Thriller)

**Failure mode prevented:** flat threat curves (every fight scene feels the same), unearned brutality (extreme violence with no narrative weight).

**Six rules:**

1. **Threat curve.** Each major confrontation is harder than the last in a specific, named way (more enemies, worse terrain, higher emotional stakes, time pressure).
2. **Justice calibration.** The hero's violence is calibrated to the enemy's threat. No one-shot kills of unarmed characters; no unprovoked escalation.
3. **Cost accumulation.** The hero accumulates physical, psychological, or relational costs across the book. They are not invincible.
4. **Civilian impact.** The hero's actions affect bystanders. Track and respect this.
5. **Antagonist competence.** The antagonist is competent, not a punchline. They make plausible decisions.
6. **Tone discipline.** Voice stays terse. No purple prose, no soliloquies, no "I had become death."

---

## §13. Organization Authenticity System (Mafia Romance)

**Five rules:** chain of command must be coherent; loyalty rules govern decisions; outsider entry is plausible; violence has organizational consequences; the cultural setting (Italian, Russian, Albanian, etc.) is rendered with specificity, not generic accents.

---

## §14. Conflict Depth System (Contemporary Romance)

**Five rules:** conflict comes from incompatible needs, not just incompatible schedules; every conflict beat tests both characters' growth, not just one; resolution requires both characters to change; black moment is earned by setup, not contrived; HEA includes a specific named change in each character's life.

---

## §15. Internal Journey System (Women's Fiction)

**Five rules:** internal change is the spine; relationships are the lens; setting deeply shapes the protagonist's options; backstory is integrated, not dumped; ending allows ambivalence (not all wins are clean).

---

## §16. Multi-Arc Balance System (Why Choose / Reverse Harem / Poly)

**Five rules:** each love interest gets equivalent on-page presence; each relationship dynamic is distinctly characterized; jealousy resolves through communication, not rivalry tropes; group scenes show relational ecology; HEA must accommodate all participants meaningfully.

---

## §17. Cozy Tone Integrity (Cozy Fantasy / Cozy LitRPG)

**Five rules:** stakes stay low-to-mid (community-scale, not world-ending); violence is mostly off-page; recurring side cast feels lived-in; setting is sensory and warm; tension comes from puzzles or relationships, not threat. For cozy LitRPG, additionally: progression is gentle and frequent, not a grind.

---

## §18. Universal Fiction Guardrail (fallback)

When the codex's genre doesn't map to any specific guardrail above, apply these four general rules every chapter:

1. **POV consistency.** Whatever POV mode the codex specifies, hold it. Never head-hop within a scene.
2. **Tense consistency.** Whatever tense the codex specifies, hold it.
3. **Character voice fingerprints.** Each named character speaks distinctly. Don't let dialogue blur.
4. **Show, don't tell.** Convert telling sentences ("She was angry") to showing ("She set the cup down too carefully.").

---

## Heat Level Calibration (overlay all systems)

Whatever heat level the codex specifies (sweet / steamy / open-door / explicit / scorching), match it precisely:

| Codex level | What this means on-page |
|---|---|
| Sweet / Closed-door | Kissing, charged touch, fade-to-black on anything more |
| Sensual / Steamy | On-page foreplay, soft-focus love scenes, anatomical specificity light |
| Open-door / Explicit | Full on-page intimate scenes, anatomical specificity, emotional context |
| Scorching | Multiple explicit scenes, kink awareness, ECIS rules apply |

If the user has overridden the heat level during the revisions phase, use the user's override, not the codex default.
