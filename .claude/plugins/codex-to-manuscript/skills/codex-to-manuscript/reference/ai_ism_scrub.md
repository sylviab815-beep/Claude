# AI-ism Scrub Reference

A manuscript that reads as AI-written almost always contains the same family of crutch phrases. This list is the catch-net. Run it during every Edit-As-You-Go pass and again during the End-of-Book pipeline.

For each banned phrase, do not just delete — rewrite the sentence to say what it actually meant.

---

## Banned phrases — emotional clichés

- a shiver ran down [their] spine
- her heart pounded / hammered / raced (default phrasing — replace with specifics)
- the weight of the world (on her shoulders / settled over him)
- she couldn't shake the feeling
- in that moment
- their eyes locked (with no further detail)
- time seemed to slow / time stood still
- the silence was deafening
- a sense of dread / unease / wrongness washed over [them]
- her breath caught
- a knot formed in [their] stomach
- his jaw tightened / clenched / ticked
- a muscle worked in his jaw
- she rolled her eyes
- he raked a hand through his hair
- she let out a breath she didn't know she was holding
- relief flooded through [them]
- her stomach dropped / fluttered
- butterflies in her stomach (unless used ironically)
- a chill ran through [her]

## Banned phrases — narration tells

- little did [they] know
- if only [they] had known
- it was as if…
- there was something about [them] that…
- [they] were unaware of…
- the truth was…
- the reality was…
- in reality
- in truth
- to be honest
- to her surprise
- much to [their] surprise

## Banned phrases — overused metaphor scaffolds

- navigated the [emotion / situation]
- a tapestry of [anything]
- the very fabric of [reality / her being / their lives]
- a kaleidoscope of [emotions]
- a symphony of [anything]
- a dance of [anything]
- a maze of [emotions / corridors / thoughts]
- ebbed and flowed
- a delicate balance
- a perfect storm
- in a delicate dance

## Banned phrases — stilted dialogue tags and adverbs

- said curiously / angrily / sadly / happily / nervously / softly (when the line carries it)
- whispered conspiratorially
- stated matter-of-factly
- exclaimed loudly
- chuckled darkly

Replace with action beats or just "said" or no tag.

## Banned phrases — generic body language

- his hands trembled
- her lips parted
- a single tear rolled down [their] cheek
- she wrapped her arms around herself
- he pinched the bridge of his nose
- she chewed her lip / her bottom lip
- he ran a tongue across his teeth
- her eyes widened
- his Adam's apple bobbed
- her cheeks flushed (unless emotionally specific)

## Banned constructions

- "…, [verb]ing…" (sentence ending in a participial phrase that adds no real information)
- Three-adjective stacks ("the cold, dark, empty room")
- Em-dash flooding (more than 3 em-dashes per page)
- Fragments stacked for effect more than two in a row
- Sentence beginnings: "And", "But", "So" (sparingly OK; never as a default rhythm)
- "I would [verb]" or "I could [verb]" used to soften narration ("I would think back on it later")
- Rhetorical questions used as narrative bridges ("How could she have known?")

## Banned setting descriptions

- "the moon hung low / cast its silver light" (unless specific to the setting)
- "the wind whispered" / "trees whispered" (unless literally part of the magic system)
- "the air was thick with…" (a clichéd opener)
- "the [city] was alive with…"
- "stars twinkled overhead"
- Any sentence starting with "The [time of day] [verb]ed" (e.g., "The morning dawned bright and clear")

## Banned overused similes

- like a deer in headlights
- like a moth to a flame
- like a kid in a candy store
- like a fish out of water
- like a bull in a china shop

Replace with original similes that fit the codex's voice register.

---

## Method

When you find a hit, don't replace word-for-word. Stop and ask:

1. **What was this sentence trying to do?** (convey shock, propulsion, dread, attraction, etc.)
2. **What specific physical or sensory detail in *this* scene could do that work?**
3. **Write that.**

Example:

| Original | Replacement |
|---|---|
| A shiver ran down her spine. | The cold from the window seam found the back of her neck. |
| He raked a hand through his hair. | He turned the cup in his hands until the handle faced him again. |
| Her heart pounded. | She could hear her pulse in her ears, fast and uneven. |
| Time seemed to slow. | Her eyes caught on the second hand on the kitchen clock — twitching, twitching, then twitching again. |

---

## Number distribution check

Authors and AI both default to certain numbers. When you write a number (age, count, distance, time of day, year), distribute across endings — don't let "seven," "twelve," or numbers ending in 5 dominate. Quick audit at the end-of-book pipeline: scan all numbers in the manuscript, ensure no single ending digit is over-represented.
