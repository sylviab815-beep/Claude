# Holistic Manuscript Polish — End-of-Pipeline Whole-Book Revision

This is the final revision pass after all other editorial passes complete (AI-ism Scrub, Developmental Edit, Beta Read, Line Edit, Proofread). Where the earlier passes operate at sentence, scene, or structural levels in isolation, this pass treats the manuscript as a **single cohesive arc** and revises for whole-book flow, consistency, and authenticity.

**Critical rule across the whole pass:** preserve chapter length and narrative weight. Do not shorten, compress, or summarize scenes to "tighten" them. The book's word count target was set in the codex; honor it.

The pass has five workstreams, applied across the full manuscript end-to-end. Apply all five, then verify the deliverables in §5 before declaring the polish complete.

---

## §1 — Structure, Pacing, and Flow

### 1.1 Preserve length

Do not shorten chapters during this pass. If a scene feels slow, address the cause (over-explaining, restating known information, redundant emotional beats) — don't cut the scene entirely.

### 1.2 Strip artificial chapter endings

Remove the following from any chapter ending:

- **Philosophical wrap-ups** — narrator stepping back to reflect on what the chapter "meant." Cut.
- **Forced cliffhangers** — manufactured suspense like "Little did she know what was waiting" or "Her world was about to change forever." Cut.
- **Artificial dramatic pauses** — single-line paragraphs that exist for visual drama only. Cut unless the line genuinely lands.
- **End-of-chapter moralizing** — narrator drawing a lesson the reader should already feel. Cut.
- **One-line cliché endings** — "Nothing would ever be the same." / "And then she ran." / "He had no idea what was coming." Cut.
- **Manufactured mic-drops** — declarative single sentences designed to feel weighty but disconnected from the scene's actual momentum. Cut.

### 1.3 End chapters on motion

Replace the cut endings with one of these:

- **Mid-scene cuts** — chapter ends with the character in the middle of an action, sentence, decision, or movement
- **Action momentum** — character is mid-action and the next chapter picks up that action
- **Decision pivot** — the character has just decided something and the next chapter shows the consequence
- **Discovery** — the character has just learned something and the next chapter shows their reaction
- **Unresolved problem** — a question, threat, or complication is now active and the reader needs to keep reading to see what happens

### 1.4 Continuous narrative motion

Each chapter must naturally pull the reader into the next. Test by reading the last paragraph of chapter N immediately followed by the first paragraph of chapter N+1. The transition should feel like a continuation, not a reset.

### 1.5 Whole-arc cohesion

Improve transitions within and between chapters so the story flows as a single arc, not as segmented beats. Watch for:

- Repeated "establishing shot" openings at the start of each chapter (sky, weather, location reset)
- Recap sentences that restate what just happened in the previous chapter
- Time-skip phrases that telegraph a passage of time without showing what happened
- Scene breaks that are just chapter breaks in disguise — combine if appropriate

## §2 — Remove AI Artifacts

### 2.1 Direct AI references

Search the entire manuscript for any of:

- "As an AI…"
- "I'm an AI" / "I am an AI"
- "As a language model"
- "I cannot" / "I don't have the ability to" (in narration, not dialogue)
- "I apologize for any confusion"

If any of these appear, cut entirely.

### 2.2 Leftover prompt language

Search for phrases that suggest the AI was responding to a prompt rather than writing in-world:

- "Write a scene where…"
- "Here is the chapter…"
- "Here's the next part of the story…"
- "In this scene, [character] does X" (where it's exposition labeling, not action)
- "Let me describe…"
- "I'll continue with…"

Cut entirely.

### 2.3 Meta-commentary

Author analysis disguised as character thought is the most common AI failure mode. Watch for:

- A character "noticing" a thematic resonance the author intended
- A character internally explaining the symbolic meaning of an object or event
- A character commenting on their own arc ("she had grown so much since…")
- A character delivering exposition the reader already has
- A character making sociological observations no real person would make in the moment

Rewrite to in-the-moment experience. The character should feel, not analyze.

### 2.4 POV breaks and perspective slips

Scan for:

- A POV character knowing something they couldn't possibly know (another character's internal state without being told)
- An "omniscient intrusion" — a sentence that pulls back to a god's-eye view in a chapter that's supposed to be close third
- A POV character describing themselves from outside (mirror tropes excepted, but use sparingly)
- POV shifts mid-scene without a clear scene break

Fix all of these so the POV stays locked.

### 2.5 Generic / robotic phrasing

Strip the following AI-default transitional phrases:

- "Additionally," "Furthermore," "Moreover," "In addition,"
- "It's worth noting," "It's important to remember,"
- "However, it's important to remember," "That said,"
- "On the other hand," "Conversely,"
- "In conclusion," "All things considered,"
- "Notably," "Specifically," "In particular,"

These belong in essays, not fiction. Replace with more natural connective tissue or simply cut.

### 2.6 Clinical emotion descriptions

Rewrite any clinical or analytical description of emotion, behavior, or motivation:

| Don't | Do |
|---|---|
| "She experienced a feeling of mild apprehension." | "Her stomach tightened." |
| "His behavior indicated a defensive posture." | "He took half a step back." |
| "The emotion she was experiencing was complex." | "She didn't know what to call it." |
| "He felt a sense of disappointment." | "His shoulders dropped." |

### 2.7 Artificial precision

Cut overly precise timing, measurements, or observations unless plot-critical:

- "Exactly 3.5 seconds passed."
- "The room measured roughly 12 feet by 14 feet."
- "She breathed in for precisely four counts."

Use this kind of precision only when the plot needs it (a coded message, a forensic detail, a martial discipline).

### 2.8 AI-style emotional labeling

Watch for "she felt something that could only be described as…" and similar constructions. The narrator is searching for the word for the feeling — that's an AI tell. Real prose either *names* the feeling directly or *renders* it through body and behavior without trying to label it.

Rewrite to lived experience.

## §3 — Dialogue and Character Voice

### 3.1 Replace stiff dialogue with authentic speech

Stilted dialogue patterns to fix:

- Complete sentences where fragments would land harder
- Formal vocabulary in casual contexts ("I am quite certain" → "I'm sure")
- No contractions in informal speech (almost everyone uses contractions; exceptions for formal characters or specific tics)
- Characters delivering exposition cleanly — real speech is messier

### 3.2 Reduce surname overuse

If two characters are intimate, friendly, or familiar — they use first names or nicknames. Surnames belong to:

- Authority figures addressed formally
- Characters who haven't earned first-name status yet
- Specific tics where one character uses surnames as a quirk

If characters are in a relationship, are family, or are close friends, they should default to first names except in specific tonal moments (anger, formality, flirtation).

### 3.3 Break repetitive call-and-response

If a dialogue exchange is just statement → response → statement → response with each line answering only the previous line, it reads as scripted. Real conversation:

- Talks past each other sometimes
- Returns to earlier threads
- Has interruptions
- Has non-sequiturs
- Has silences

Unless the rhythm is intentional and character-driven (a chess match, a deposition, a careful negotiation), break the pattern.

### 3.4 Break overly polished exchanges

If an exchange feels scripted, add:

- **Interruptions** — one character cuts the other off mid-sentence
- **Overlaps** — both characters speak at once
- **Pauses** — beats where neither speaks
- **Conversational messiness** — false starts, "um," "I mean," "wait, no"
- **Physical action mid-line** — a character looks away, picks something up, walks past

### 3.5 Maintain distinct voices

Each character should sound different. Test by covering the dialogue tags and reading a page of dialogue — can you tell who's speaking from voice alone? If not, the voices have collapsed. Common fingerprints to lean on:

- Sentence length (long-winded vs. terse)
- Vocabulary register (academic vs. street vs. period-specific)
- Speech tics (one character uses "you know," another never does; one uses "fine," another "okay")
- Subject preferences (what each character notices, brings up, returns to)
- Body-language patterns rendered through dialogue (fidgety vs. still, hands-talkers vs. statue-still)

### 3.6 Voice consistency across the manuscript

Read each character's dialogue end-to-end (or scan it). Voice should not drift across the book. If a character starts the book with one fingerprint and ends with a different one without an arc-driven reason, restore consistency.

## §4 — Narrative Authenticity and Immersion

### 4.1 Replace forced metaphors

Cut any metaphor that:

- Pulls focus to its own cleverness
- Uses an image incongruous with the POV character's worldview
- Repeats an earlier metaphor in slightly different form (lazy variation)
- Strains so far the reader has to work to parse it

If a metaphor doesn't immediately clarify or deepen the moment, replace with direct rendering.

### 4.2 Simplify inflated sentence structures

Watch for:

- Sentences with three or more dependent clauses where two would do
- Padding constructions: "the fact that," "in spite of the fact that," "due to the fact that"
- Hedging stacks: "she seemed to almost begin to feel something like…"
- Adverb clusters: "very, extremely, completely, entirely…"

Cut padding. Pick the strongest clause. Trust the verb.

### 4.3 Remove author-as-character observations

If a sentence reads as the author observing the character, rewrite as the character observing themselves or the world. The reader should never feel an author standing behind the character explaining them.

### 4.4 Cut filler connectors

In addition to the AI-style transitional phrases in §2.5, watch for:

- "And so," at the start of paragraphs
- "Suddenly," doing the work of actually rendering surprise
- "Just then," to announce timing
- "Finally," to announce the end of waiting

These are scaffolding the writer used while drafting and forgot to remove.

### 4.5 Lock all sense-detail to POV

Every sensory detail, interpretation, and observation must come through the active POV character's awareness and limitations:

- They can't see what's behind them
- They can't smell what they're nose-blind to
- They can't hear what's outside earshot
- They can't know what another character is thinking
- They can't know facts they haven't been told
- Their interpretations are colored by their personality, background, and emotional state

If a sentence breaks this, fix it.

## §5 — Deliverables and Confirmation

After applying §1–§4 across the full manuscript, verify:

### 5.1 Output

- Fully revised manuscript with all §1–§4 fixes applied consistently from beginning to end
- No chapter has been shortened — every chapter retains its codex-target length, depth, and narrative weight
- Every chapter ends on motion, not on cliché or manufactured drama
- The Edit Log has a "Holistic Polish Pass" section listing the major changes made (especially structural, pacing, and dialogue revisions)

### 5.2 Confirmation checklist

Verify each before declaring the polish complete:

- [ ] Manuscript word count is at or above the codex target
- [ ] No chapter ends on a cliché or manufactured mic-drop
- [ ] No direct AI references anywhere in the manuscript
- [ ] No prompt-language artifacts ("Write a scene where…")
- [ ] All transitional AI-isms removed ("Additionally," "Furthermore," etc.)
- [ ] All clinical emotion labels rewritten to lived experience
- [ ] Dialogue feels spoken — contractions, fragments, interruptions, conversational messiness
- [ ] Each major character has a distinct, consistent voice across the book
- [ ] All sense-detail is grounded in POV character awareness
- [ ] No omniscient intrusions or perspective slips
- [ ] Reading the last paragraph of every chapter immediately followed by the first paragraph of the next produces continuous narrative motion

### 5.3 Examples

Append 3-5 short before/after examples to the Edit Log, covering:

- One structural / pacing fix (e.g., a removed chapter-ending mic-drop replaced with mid-scene momentum)
- One dialogue fix (e.g., a stilted exchange rewritten with interruptions)
- One AI-artifact fix (e.g., a clinical emotion description rewritten to lived experience)
- One voice fix (e.g., a character dialogue passage that drifted, restored to fingerprint)
- One immersion fix (e.g., an omniscient intrusion repaired to POV-locked)

These examples become reference for future books in the same series and prove to the customer that the work was done.

---

## When to run this pass

- **Built into the main pipeline:** as Pass 6 of the end-of-book editorial pipeline (after Proofread). Final pass before the final integrity gate and delivery.
- **Standalone command:** `/polish-manuscript` runs only this pass on any existing manuscript markdown the customer supplies. Useful for customers who already have a finished draft and want only the polish.

## What this pass does NOT do

- Does not change the plot
- Does not add or remove chapters
- Does not shorten the manuscript
- Does not swap characters or settings
- Does not re-genre the book
- Does not rewrite intimate scenes (those come from the writing pass with ECIS rules; if heat level needs changing, do that during user revisions phase, not here)
